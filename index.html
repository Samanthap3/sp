RESEARCH

Human-AI Interaction

Undergraduate researcher at UIUC's Hands-On Intelligence Lab, studying how people co-create with AI tools during hands-on making.

PILOT STUDY

Human–AI Interactions in Craftwork Environments

Workshop: A design-based research study using a 2-hour e-textile patch workshop to observe how 10-15 participants of varying skill levels co-create with AI tools and what happens to creativity, decision-making, and confidence in the makerprocess.

Role: Research Assistant under Professor Kyungwon Koh and Professor Christine Marcelino, helping shape research methodology, questions, and target subjects, and co-organized the workshop with a 7-person research team.

Early insights: AI functions as a supportive collaborator rather than a replacement, participants often turn to peers over AI in maker settings, and visual demonstration outpaces chat-based guidance for learning.

Next Steps: I am continuing this reaserch by focusing on where AI benefits makers most in the design process and using human centered design to create a way for makers to communicate with ai through the physical creation process rather than words.
